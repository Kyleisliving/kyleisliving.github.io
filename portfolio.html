Summaware

Lead UI/UX Designer — Summaware.com

Summaware is an all-in-one ERP and media workflow platform built to replace the fragmented software stacks that news publishers had been duct-taping together for years. The platform consolidates five core business systems — Editorial (CMS), Circulation & Subscription Billing, Advertising (CRM), Production & Layout Automation, and Business Intelligence & Analytics — into a single unified experience.

As Head of UI/UX Design, my challenge was making complex, cross-departmental workflows feel approachable for the people using them every day. I owned the design process end-to-end: translating messy, multi-system logic into clean, intuitive flows; presenting decisions to key stakeholders; and working closely with dev teams to make sure the builds matched the vision.

Bangor Daily News

UI/UX Designer — Client Project

Brought in as a contract designer alongside my work at Summaware, I was tasked with rethinking Bangor Daily News's subscription purchase flow from the ground up. This wasn't a light refresh — I built the entire design system for this project: style guide, component library, and the full end-to-end workflow.

The design went through several rounds of stakeholder review, and I iterated accordingly at each stage — balancing their editorial identity with a purchase experience that felt modern and frictionless. The goal was to make subscribers feel like they were joining something meaningful, not just checking out a cart.

Criterion Channel

UI/UX Design — Personal Project

This one started with a simple premise: as a designer, I believe the best work comes from genuinely understanding what it feels like to need something. Criterion Channel is an app I use regularly and love — so when I noticed friction in the search and discovery experience, I couldn't help but ask what would I actually want here?

I put myself fully in the user's seat and designed the experience I wished existed — a more refined, intuitive search flow that feels as cinematic as the films it surfaces. No client brief, no stakeholder approval — just a designer treating a product they care about as a design problem worth solving.
